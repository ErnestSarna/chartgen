# Charting lessons from beyond Clone Hero

*A synthesis of 11 research reports mining osu!, StepMania/ITG/DDR, Beat Saber, Japanese arcade rhythm games, Rocksmith, Fortnite Festival/RBN/YARG/Encore, academic chart-generation literature, minimalist rhythm games, academic guitar-tablature/fingering research, karaoke/vocal-alignment research, and MIR structure-segmentation research — for the chartgen maintainers.*

---

## 1. Executive summary: findings that should actually change chartgen

Ranked by (evidence strength × likely value) / implementation effort.

### 1.1 Cap inter-tier density *jumps*, not just each tier's ratio-to-Expert in isolation
**Evidence:** BandFuse: Rock Legends — reviewers repeatedly flag "sharp difficulty spikes between tiers" as the game's signature complaint (GameSpot, Wikipedia). Rocksmith's community tool DDC independently enforces "max 4 new notes/chords per level-transition" as a hard anti-spike cap. osu!mania scales chord-size caps as a *fraction of available lanes* per tier rather than an absolute count, and osu! standard requires spacing/consistency itself (not just density) to shrink smoothly tier-to-tier. Three unrelated domains converged on the same failure mode and the same fix shape.
**Concrete change:** Add a validation pass that measures the delta between adjacent tiers (density, sustain share, chord rate) and flags/caps deltas that exceed some bound, independent of whether each tier individually hits its target ratio (chartgen's 81/58/41%). Cheap, mechanical, directly targets a documented competitor failure mode.

### 1.2 Adopt the Overdrive/Star Power density formula as a cross-checked baseline
**Evidence:** RBN/C3's authoring docs specify **"1 Overdrive Phrase per 40 beats"** (≈1 per 10 measures in 4/4, ≈1 measure long, none in the last ~8 measures, drum-solo cap of 4 phrases). YARG's own 2020s style guide, written independently ~10+ years later with no connection to RBN, states "Star power phrases should span at least two beats, and should be placed about 10 measures apart" — the same number, arrived at separately.
**Concrete change:** If chartgen's SP placement logic doesn't already check against "~1 phrase per 10 measures (4/4), ~1 measure long, none in the outro," add it as a sanity-check baseline. This is about as close to a validated cross-game constant as this whole research effort found.

### 1.3 Formalize playability as a *transition/state-machine* property, not a per-note score
**Evidence:** This is the single most cross-corroborated principle in the entire research set. BSMG's Beat Saber wiki states it as doctrine: "think about the relation between sequences of notes, not about individual hits." ITG's community doctrine: crossovers "must resolve properly so the player doesn't have to guess," forced doublesteps are named anti-patterns because they "interrupt flow." osu!'s taiko/mania rules require rest placement *relative to phrase boundaries*, not just aggregate density. CustomsForge's binding 2025 standard explicitly prioritizes hand-ergonomics/fingering correctness *above* difficulty-curve correctness. Academically, Heijink & Meulenbroek's 2002 motion-capture study and the 2025 Fretting-Transformer paper — 23 years apart, unrelated fields — independently converge on the same three-axis cost model: position/stretch, hand-repositioning distance, and a *rolling average position* anchor (not just previous-note distance).
**Concrete change:** If chartgen's hand-movement/playability simulation currently scores note-to-note transitions only against the immediately preceding note, add a "deviation from rolling local hand-position average" term (Tuohy & Potter's validated addition) and an explicit binary "is the resulting hand/pick state unambiguous" check (ITG's ambiguity-resolution doctrine, BeatSaver's `bs-parity`-style state tracker) — independent of the existing density/difficulty score.

### 1.4 Treat low-tier (Easy) generation as its own problem with its own reference data — not a thinned Expert
**Evidence:** The ISMIR 2023 GOCT paper (osu!mania 4K) reports the model "fails catastrophically on generating low-difficulty charts," attributed explicitly to sparse charts being underrepresented in training data. Dance Dance Convolution shows the identical pattern quantitatively: F-score^c 0.844 on hard charts vs. only **0.389 on the lowest difficulty**. osu!'s and Beat Saber's ranking criteria both encode *separate, qualitative* rules per tier (which move-types are permitted) rather than one formula scaled by a multiplier. GenéLive!'s production data shows professionally-authored tiers are near-perfect supersets of each other (97–99% inclusion rate) — a property that requires deliberate design, not emergent thinning.
**Concrete change:** Audit whether chartgen's Easy-tier calibration data are genuine dedicated Easy references or downsampled Experts (mirrors chartgen's own documented tier-ratio recalibration history). Compute an "inclusion rate" metric (`|easier_tier ∩ harder_tier| / |easier_tier|`) against the 800-chart set as a new, cheap diagnostic.

### 1.5 The single biggest lever in the best documented commercial deployment was an explicit, deterministic beat-position feature — not model complexity
**Evidence:** GenéLive! (KLab's production auto-charter, live since 2020 in *Love Live!* and *Utano Princesama*) found via ablation that a trivial, hand-computed trinary "beat/downbeat/nothing" signal was **"the most significant element in the present model"** — removing it dropped Beginner-tier F1 from 0.866 to 0.803 and crushed low-data titles' Beginner F1 from 0.686 to 0.452. This outweighed architecture depth. The same paper's multi-scale conv stack (32/512/2048/4096ms) captured phrase-repetition (verse/chorus mirroring) and produced a statistically significant improvement the artists confirmed matched their own intent.
**Concrete change:** This validates chartgen's existing beat-grid-first architecture (don't second-guess it). The actionable extension: anywhere chartgen infers rhythmic position indirectly through timing math, prefer an explicit categorical "which subdivision / is this a downbeat" feature fed directly into the rule. Separately, consider detecting repeated N-bar phrases in the beat grid and deliberately mirroring note-placement decisions across the repeat — a rule-based analogue of GenéLive!'s learned behavior.

### 1.6 Quantify chartgen's own onset-detection ceiling the way STRUM did
**Evidence:** STRUM (arXiv:2605.12135, an unreviewed but methodologically careful preprint targeting Clone Hero/YARG directly) measured that **only 89.0% of 39,136 real community-chart drum events have any detectable audio onset within ±100ms at all** — the rest are charted sustain/filler notes with no audible attack, or charter-quantization choices. This sets a hard, unavoidable ceiling on any onset-detector's achievable recall against real community charts, independent of model quality.
**Concrete change:** Run the equivalent measurement on chartgen's own reference library/800-chart set: what fraction of real charted notes correspond to a detectable Basic Pitch onset within some tolerance? This separates "our transcription needs work" from "some fraction of real charts are inherently unreachable from acoustics alone," which matters for deciding where further transcription effort is worth it.

### 1.7 Use paired per-song statistical testing for calibration decisions, not aggregate-only deltas
**Evidence:** STRUM's own ablation methodology (paired per-song Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across a 7-component drum pipeline) found that **3 of 7 hand-tuned heuristics showed a real, statistically significant effect (p≤0.003) while 4 showed none (|ΔF1|≤0.001, p≥0.29)** — despite each having been individually justified against specific dev-set failure cases during development. Their explicit recommendation: "future autocharter evaluations include paired per-song statistics rather than headline-only F1 numbers," because aggregate metrics hide both false negatives (real per-song effects that cancel in aggregate) and false positives (components that "worked" on a small dev set but don't generalize).
**Concrete change:** When chartgen recalibrates tier ratios or heuristics against the 800-chart set (per its own git history of recalibration commits), add paired per-song significance testing as standard practice — cheap to implement, directly guards against exactly the kind of silent regression/no-op tuning chartgen's iterative recalibration history is at risk of.

### 1.8 Gate the required difficulty-tier floor by song length, and thin using musically-informed removal order, not uniform decimation
**Evidence:** osu!'s Ranking Criteria codify that short songs (<3:30 drain) cannot require a lowest-tier chart harder than Normal — i.e., a short/simple song is not force-fitted with a padded, oversimplified Easy chart. Separately, RBN's fully-specified chord-reduction ladder (Expert→Hard: drop Green+Orange 3-note chords first; Hard→Medium: drop all 3-note chords, then specific 2-note voicings; Medium→Easy: no chords at all — with an explicit consistency rule that every color must have ≥1 gem at every lower tier) shows *which specific notes* survive matters more than *how many*. Rocksmith's own DDC tool names its removal strategies (`ddc_dd_chords_remover`, `ddc_dd_chords_protector`) rather than leaving them implicit.
**Concrete change (two related, cheap wins):** (a) don't force-generate a full 5-tier ladder for very short/simple songs if it produces an unrepresentative Easy; (b) cross-check whether chartgen's density-ratio tiers are being hit via "fewer notes generally" or "the specific right notes" — RBN's fully-specified qualitative ladder is a concrete reference to validate against.

## 2. Cross-game principles — the closest thing to universal truths

These are patterns that multiple *independent* charting cultures (different games, different eras, different physical instruments) converged on without copying each other. Independent convergence is the strongest form of evidence available in this domain, since almost none of these games' internal difficulty formulas are actually documented — conventions that appear across unrelated communities are more trustworthy than any single community's stated rule.

1. **Difficulty tiers should be strict supersets of each other wherever possible.** GenéLive!'s production data: 97–99% inclusion rate between adjacent tiers in professionally-authored charts (vs. 60–98%, noisier, in community StepMania data). RBN: "anything that is a chord in Hard should be a chord in Medium... unless there is a super special circumstance," plus every lane color used in Expert must appear at least once in every lower tier. This is a documented property of *professional* charting specifically, not an academic simplification.

2. **Playability is evaluated over transitions/sequences, never isolated per-note.** BSMG (Beat Saber): "think about the relation between sequences of notes, not about individual hits." ITG: crossovers must "resolve properly," ambiguity in "when to reset" is a named defect independent of raw playability. osu!mania: rest placement must fall *between phrases*, not just meet an aggregate ratio. osu!taiko: color choice must have "local contrast" from the immediately preceding/following note. All are state-machine constraints, not point checks.

3. **Density/intensity should track the music's own energy contour, not a fixed independent template.** osu!'s Simplified Ranking Criteria: "intense parts have more rhythm density... show gradual progression" when the song's own intensity changes gradually. Rhythm Doctor's design doctrine: build difficulty directly from the song's intensity graph. osu!taiko's ranking rules state the same principle for a completely different instrument model.

4. **Rest/breathing room is its own tier-scaled axis, independent of average density.** osu!taiko codifies *mandatory* minimum rest frequency per tier (Kantan/Futsuu: ≥2–3 beat rest at least every 32–36 beats; Oni: only ≥1 beat every 16–20). RBN mandates no Overdrive phrase in the last ~8 measures and no Unison in the last ~12 — a structural "cool-down zone." Netflix's subtitle spec enforces minimum/maximum event durations and minimum inter-event gaps for exactly the same "don't leave the reader with no breathing room, and don't leave near-zero-but-nonzero gaps" reason.

5. **A chart is inherently a subjective interpretation — validate via relative ranking/anchoring, not absolute-value matching.** ITG's block scale is explicitly anchor-based (compare new charts against known references), not formulaic, and its own wiki calls difficulty "a subjective concept." The StepMania ordinal-regression paper (IJCAI 2023) found near-perfect (>99%) cross-pack agreement on *pairwise ranking* while absolute-difficulty metrics (WAE) degraded badly across differently-labeled packs — up to a full difficulty-tier offset between conventions for equivalent objective difficulty. osu!'s entire Beatmap Nominator system institutionalizes human subjective judgment rather than claiming one objectively-correct transcription exists.

6. **One difficulty scalar is not enough — multi-axis profiling is a repeatedly reinvented pattern.** DDR's Groove Radar (Stream/Voltage/Air/Freeze/Chaos), IIDX's Notes Radar (NOTES/PEAK/SCRATCH/CHORD/CHARGE/SOF-LAN), SDVX's Effect Radar (NOTES/PEAK/TSUMAMI/ONE-HAND/HAND-TRIP/TRICKY), BeatLeader's three parallel axes (Pass/Tech/Acc, precisely because "a map can be easy-to-pass-but-hard-to-tech, or vice versa"), and Chunithm's separate kanji-attribute-tag-plus-stars — five independent product lines across three companies, none citing each other's formulas, all landing on "expose several named axes instead of one number."

7. **Placement (when) is a fundamentally more tractable ML/heuristic problem than selection (what).** Across three published generations of dance-game chart generators (DDC → DDCL → ITGPT): step-placement F1 climbed 0.7317→0.7598→0.8022 while step-selection accuracy only reached 0.4523→0.5533→0.5908, with ITGPT's own authors calling selection "a more difficult and creative task." The same split appears in TCP's osu! work (beat-snapping recovers most of the placement gap cheaply; note-type/pattern selection remains the hard part) and in GenéLive!'s own framing. This is a structural property of the *problem*, not a symptom of insufficient model size — a caution against expecting the "choice" side of chartgen's pipeline (HOPO/technique/lane selection) to close the same way pure timing does.

8. **Sustains/holds should be additive polish, never load-bearing for a pattern's validity.** ScoreSaber's arc rule: "notes connected by an arc must be rankable even if the arc was removed." RBN's "unwhammyable sustains" anti-pattern ties sustain-worthiness to actual player experience during the hold, not just note-onset math.

9. **Curate a small, quality-filtered reference/training set rather than mining broadly.** osumapper's explicit lesson ("don't train with every single map... that's not how machine learning works," curated to a narrow 5.0–6.5★ band from trusted mappers), TaikoNation's deliberate 100-chart curated-from-a-ranking-gated-database training set, and Macrae & Dixon's finding that community tab popularity/rating does *not* correlate with transcription accuracy, all independently support chartgen's own 800-chart genre-balanced calibration approach over indiscriminate scraping.

10. **Automated generation should be a draft that a QA/human-review phase can still overrule — never a single-shot pipeline with no separate review stage.** GenéLive! explicitly kept humans in the loop for franchise/business/emotional decisions even after halving authoring time. Rocksmith+'s ARCHI tool is always reviewed/corrected by a human Music Analyst. Rock Band 4's Rhythm Authors studio allocates a full 50% of project time to QA as parity with authoring time, with an automated linter run before any human pass. ScoreSaber/osu! both formalize a Rules-vs-Guidelines two-tier bar. For chartgen (fully automated, no human-in-the-loop by design) the transferable residue is architectural: keep a distinct, separately-versioned rule-based validation/linting pass, separate from the generation heuristics themselves.

## 3. Per-domain highlights (compressed)

### osu! (all 4 modes) — the only rhythm game with a formal, enforced public ranking standard
- Two-tier **Rules vs. Guidelines** system (Rules = must never break; Guidelines = break with justification) — [Ranking criteria hub](https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Ranking_criteria).
- Difficulty floor gated by **song drain time**: <3:30 → lowest tier can't exceed Normal; 3:30–4:15 → can't exceed Hard; 4:15–5:00 → can't exceed Insane; every tier needs ≥30s drain.
- osu!mania: dead lanes (a column never used) are unrankable; chord size capped as a *fraction of available columns*, not an absolute count; long-note minimum duration and post-release gap shrink together, tier-paired.
- osu!taiko: don/kat (color) assignment must track pitch/timbre, with a mandatory local-contrast rule against repeating the same color unless the audio actually repeats; per-tier run-length caps given as an explicit table (Muzukashii max 5-note 1/4 runs, Oni max 9).
- Star rating formula is **never prose-documented** (`<!-- TODO -->` placeholder on the official wiki) — only exists as C# source code, decomposed into orthogonal "skill" strain curves (aim/speed/reading) combined into one scalar. Pattern (decompose into decaying local strain, then combine) is portable; the specific axes are not.
- AI mappers: osumapper's curated-training-data lesson; Mapperatorinator/osu-diffusion treat difficulty as one conditioning token to a single model rather than training N separate models per tier.
- ISMIR 2023 (Yi, Lee & Lee, arXiv:2311.13687, osu!mania 4K): beat-relative framing empirically necessary (87.9% vs 70.0% onset F1, beat-aligned vs. absolute-time, with the latter drifting over a song) — validates chartgen's beat-grid architecture; low-difficulty generation fails catastrophically due to sparse training coverage.

### StepMania / DDR / ITG (dance games)
- ITG block scale (1–13, later unbounded to 32/20) is explicitly anchor-based/subjective, not formulaic; DDR's Groove Radar is a **5-axis vector** (Stream/Voltage/Air/Freeze/Chaos) with documented knee points (300, 600, 55 notes/min).
- Stamina/footspeed is a second, orthogonal, community-invented rating axis independent of the official number, normalized to 16th-note-equivalent BPM.
- **Breakdown notation** — a compact plaintext language (`24/6-6-6(32)64`) describing a chart's macro stream/rest structure, auto-rendered by modern themes as an overlay on the density graph.
- Ben Blum's ML rating study: a 3-feature SVM (total stream measures, %stream, BPM) beat an RNN fed raw sequences (53.7% exact-bucket accuracy vs ~47%) — density-driven difficulty saturates on simple features; author explicitly flags **technical (pattern-variety) difficulty as out of scope and structurally different**.
- ITG's Technical Notation (`XO`/`BR`/`FS`/`DS`/`JA`/etc., with `+`/`-` intensity suffixes) — a formal embeddable pattern-vocabulary tag system, 20+ years durable.
- Forced-doublestep doctrine: "avoided in almost all cases... interrupts flow, costs more energy," and crossovers must "resolve properly so the player doesn't have to guess."
- ML lineage: Dance Dance Convolution (ICML 2017) → DDCL (ConvLSTM, 2025, arXiv:2507.01644) → ITGPT (2026, arXiv:2607.14148). Two-stage placement/selection architecture consistently wins over joint single-model approaches; a joint model is explicitly called "impractical" by ITGPT's own authors (2^256 combinatorial output space). Recurrent/conv locality specifically beat a global-attention transformer for **hold/freeze-note accuracy** in the one paper that compared them directly (DDCL 0.4115 vs. ITGPT-family 0.3782).

### Beat Saber
- **Flow** doctrine (BSMG wiki): evaluate transitions, not isolated hits; forehand/backhand asymmetry is named and codified; "vision" is a first-class constraint independent of difficulty ("do not sacrifice vision for pattern setup").
- **Parity** (`bs-parity` tool): formal alternation rule with numeric angle bands (0–45° = reset/bad, 90° = questionable, 135–180° = proper flow); "reset" is a named, defined failure mode; sanctioned reset mechanisms (bomb resets positioned at 2/3–3/4 of the gap) vs. explicitly disavowed ones (parity breaks used to fake drum-rebound feel — "imaginary computer blocks don't [rebound]").
- ScoreSaber Ranking Criteria: explicit Rules-vs-Guidelines split; hard numeric rules (45° same-swing angle ceiling, swing-speed-consistency requirement, 20ms min bomb separation, arc multiplier bounded 0.1–1.5 with the "must be rankable even if the arc is removed" invariant); explicit anti-overmapping ("a single sound cannot be represented by multiple notes unless in one swing") *and* anti-undermapping rules as separate, both-directions-checkable constraints.
- BiRating (arXiv:2502.19742): infers difficulty purely from aggregate player score data (bipartite `score ≈ skill × ease` model), found existing content-based difficulty estimates diverge most from outcome-based ones specifically in the **mid-difficulty range**.
- AI mappers (Beat Sage, DeepSaber, InfernoSaber) fail specifically at flow/parity (global coherence), not local pattern-matching — and BeatSaver's own Terms of Service impose a **90-day auto-deletion policy on AI-generated maps** as a structural, platform-level rejection mechanism.

### Japanese arcade rhythm games (Taiko no Tatsujin, IIDX, DDR, SDVX, maimai, Chunithm, DJMAX)
- **TaikoNation** (arXiv:2107.12506): the field's clearest evidence that pattern *congruence* (motif reuse, "patterning") is separable from onset accuracy and is what human ratings actually reward — 92.5–94.1% overlap with human pattern vocabulary vs. DDC's 78.7–83.2%, at statistically tied onset accuracy.
- KNN-by-measure template-matching (IEEE MIPR 2020) as an alternative to generative modeling: retrieve and stitch whole human-authored measure-patterns from acoustically-similar songs.
- IIDX difficulty scale was **renumbered five times in six years** (1999–2005) with no public rationale disclosed — precedent for periodic recalibration/rescaling rather than infinite stretching. DJMAX forked its hardest tier (SC) into its own separate scale (2022) rather than continuing to stretch a shared ladder.
- Three independent Konami product lines (DDR, IIDX, SDVX) converged on hexagonal multi-axis radar profiles with **undisclosed formulas** — the convergence on the concept is the signal, not any recoverable math.
- maimai DX/Chunithm: internal decimal "chart constants" are never publicly disclosed by SEGA — professional difficulty rating is empirically hand-tuned by internal playtesters, then reverse-engineered by the community. Validates chartgen's own measured-against-real-charts approach as matching, not falling short of, industry practice.
- Chunithm WORLD'S END: a kanji "attribute" tag plus a small star scale — explicit official precedent for separating "what kind of challenge" from "how hard."
- NAOKI (DDR) interview: Konami-original songs are composed "gameplay-first" — chart quality partly reflects composer/charter alignment unavailable to a tool charting pre-existing songs.

### Rocksmith & real-instrument games
- Rocksmith's dynamic-difficulty **patent** (US 9,839,852) names note-frequency as the one explicit axis and ties adjustment to localized weak-passage detection + isolated drilling — but was **invalidated as an abstract idea** ("no different from... a music teacher"), confirming there's no hidden algorithm to mine, only unpublished tuning data.
- Community DDC tool: phrase-length-based leveling (2/3/4 measures), named ramp-up strategies (`ddc_dd_chords_remover` etc.), max-4-new-notes-per-transition anti-spike cap; DDCImprover exists purely to patch known DDC failure modes (single-level phrases, dropped fret-hand positions, off-beat difficulty boundaries).
- **CustomsForge's binding 2025 standard**: manual phrase/FHP/fingering required ("don't rely on auto-generated" stated twice as a hard rule), sustain rule (≥1/32 gap before next note), and — critically — **Dynamic Difficulty is the one field explicitly allowed to stay auto-generated**, i.e. the community's own priority order is playability-correctness first, difficulty-curve second.
- **ARCHI** (Rocksmith+, Ubisoft dev diary): ML tool gives an "unverified" first-pass chord chart, always reviewed by a human Music Analyst; explicitly still fails on syncopated genres (funk, reggae) and ambiguous time signatures; reused, limited chord-shape vocabulary is deliberate so skill transfers song-to-song.
- Rocksmith 1→2014 design reversal: continuous "Combo" (always play the interesting part) replaced by strict Lead/Rhythm/Bass paths — controversial, never fully resolved even by the original team. A documented case where "which single part survives" has no clean universal answer.
- BandFuse's reviewer-documented "sharp difficulty spikes between tiers" (see §1.1).
- Yousician's open dataset (1,000 learners, 10M+ note-level evaluations) — unexploited but validates note-grain, large-corpus calibration as a sound methodology independent of chartgen's own domain.

### Modern plastic-instrument games (RBN/C3, Rock Band 4, YARG, Encore, Fortnite Festival)
- **RBN/C3 authoring docs** — the most fully-specified charting spec found anywhere in this research: exact HOPO tick threshold (~170 ticks, just above an 8th-note triplet at 160), tempo-relative sustain eligibility (dotted-8th at ≥100 BPM, plain 8th below), the full chord-reduction ladder (§1.8), the 40-beats-per-OD-phrase formula (§1.2), and vocal-phrase rules including a **600ms inter-phrase-gap trigger** for structurally significant silence.
- Rhythm Authors (RB4 DLC studio): 50/50 authoring/QA time split, automated pre-submission linter, majority-consensus revision process.
- **YARG**: fret-range-limited lower difficulties (Easy=3 lanes, Medium=4, convention-based); independently arrived at the same ~10-measure OD spacing as RBN; documents the *mechanical* (not stylistic) reason taps exist — per-note vs. per-run miss recovery; Chart Awards reward *completeness* (coverage across instruments/difficulties), a metric orthogonal to note-quality.
- **Encore**: confirms it's a distinct open-source game (chartgen's calibration-set "Encore-labeled" charts refer to this), CH-file-compatible; explicitly defers to RBN's spec for practice sections, 15 years later; introduces "lift" notes (release-triggered) with no CH equivalent.
- **Fortnite Festival**: regular Lead/Bass use press-or-release "Lift Notes" (no strum, no HOPO) as a structurally different instrument model from Pro Lead/Bass (classic fret+strum+HOPO); no public authoring rubric exists for either mode.

### Academic chart/beatmap-generation literature (2017–2026)
- **DDC → GenéLive! → TaikoNation → TCP** lineage covered above and in §1.5/§2.7.
- **GenéLive!** (AAAI-23, arXiv:2202.12823) is the field's one real deployed commercial system: **halved authoring cost (40h→20h/song)** across 110 shipped songs; a case study shows 21/22 auto-generated notes kept unmodified by human artists in one excerpt (precision 0.72/recall 0.91 over a full 60-bar song); explicitly did *not* attempt full automation — kept humans for franchise decisions and choreography/emotional-beat ties "harder to compile into numerical data."
- **STRUM** (unreviewed preprint, arXiv:2605.12135) — only system targeting CH/YARG directly; onset-ceiling quantification (§1.6), paired-Wilcoxon ablation methodology (§1.7), explicit "operating envelope" screening (rejects 37% of candidate songs as unsuitable up front rather than reporting misleading aggregate numbers on unsuitable inputs); per-class drum accuracy as low as 0.19 for CH's own blue-lane (high-tom/ride) conflation — a format-level ceiling no model can fix.
- Ordinal-regression StepMania paper (IJCAI 2023, arXiv:2301.09485): absolute difficulty labels don't transfer across authoring conventions (WAE degrades sharply cross-pack); pairwise ranking agreement is near-perfect (>99%) — validate on relative ordering, not absolute scale-matching. Also: ML rankings beat *hobbyist* author labels on human-perception agreement, but lose to *professionally*-authored (ITG) labels — professional human judgment remains the gold standard where it exists.
- Robo-Saber (Eurographics 2026, arXiv:2602.18319): physically-simulated VR player-movement generation from map geometry, for automated playtesting before human playtesting — an independent validation that simulating physical execution (not just structural/acoustic analysis) is a recognized necessary complement, i.e. external confirmation of chartgen's own hand-movement simulation approach; suggests simulating *per-tier* skill-conditioned movement rather than one fixed playability model.

### Minimalist/unconventional rhythm games (sparse-tier lessons)
- **Rhythm Doctor** (1 button): "the song itself is the level" — difficulty built by layering a small vocabulary of rhythmic primitives (base pulse, 2-beat patterns, held beats, skipped beats), explicitly compared to music-pedagogy etudes (Hanon, Suzuki method); each beat-type must "feel clear and distinct" from the others.
- **Crypt of the NecroDancer**: started with a strict ~20%-of-beat timing window, then *widened it to ~100% leeway* after finding "the times when you are least accurate are the times when you are most stressed" — strictness under pressure reads as unfairness, not difficulty; ships a "leaky integrator" autocalibration that re-centers the game's internal beat to the player's felt tempo.
- **osu!taiko ranking criteria** (2-input game, closest structural analog to a sparse ladder): explicit numeric minimum-spacing-per-tier *and* mandatory-rest-frequency-per-tier as two separate, both-codified axes (§2.4); color/timbre-to-input assignment must track pitch with local-contrast anti-repetition.
- **Friday Night Funkin'**: difficulty varies three independently-documented knobs — density, pattern complexity, and *scroll speed decoupled from actual rhythmic complexity* — a clean example of separating a purely visual/legibility lever from a musical-complexity lever.
- StepMania ML difficulty research (open-source replication): **peak/local burst density, not mean density, is the better difficulty predictor** once controlled — charts concentrating difficulty into short dense bursts against a lower baseline are rated harder than the same average density spread evenly.
- Hi-Fi Rush (documented intent, thin on mechanism): explicit design rejection of "rhythm as constraint" — reward approximate timing so players feel "lifted up," not punished, echoing NecroDancer's finding in an unrelated genre.

### Academic guitar tablature/fingering research (real-fretboard MIR)
- **Convergent 3-axis fingering-cost model, 23 years apart, two unrelated methodologies**: Heijink & Meulenbroek's 2002 motion-capture study (hand position, finger span, repositioning frequency) and the 2025 Fretting-Transformer's independently-learned cost signal (fret stretch, position-height "locality," vertical string-span movement) — strong validation these are the right minimal feature set for *any* fretted-instrument playability model, including chartgen's abstracted 5-lane one.
- **Tuohy & Potter's GA** (ICMC 2005): validated 3-term fitness function — fretted-note count, adjacent-note fret distance, and **distance from the running average fret position of surrounding notes** (a distinct "hand-anchor" term missing from simple adjacent-distance costs) — outperformed commercial auto-tab software of its era. The same authors explicitly *abandoned* per-finger biomechanical tracking (the theoretically "more correct" model) for generation purposes because it was "very difficult to capture accurately" — a documented diminishing-returns precedent.
- **Radisavljevic & Driessen** (ICMC 2004): fit a fingering cost function's weights *from real tablature via gradient descent* rather than hand-tuning them — a directly reusable methodology for calibrating chartgen's own playability weights against its 800-chart set.
- **TabCNN's Tablature Disambiguation Rate (TDR)**: a metric that separately scores "is the pitch/timing correct" from "among multiple valid fingerings for that correct pitch, did the model pick what a human would pick" — directly portable as a QA metric for chartgen's lane/technique assignment (HOPO vs. strum, which lane for a chord).
- MIDI-to-Tab MLM paper (arXiv:2408.05024): a 15-guitarist playability user study found even a well-fit model (matching pitch/fingering distribution closely) scored 6.04±2.67 vs. ground truth's 7.45±2.62 on subjective playability — matching the *distribution* doesn't close the *felt* playability gap, and cheap bound-clamping post-processing didn't fix it.
- Statistical Piano Reduction paper (arXiv:1808.05006): reframes a difficulty ladder as "maximize fidelity to the original subject to a difficulty constraint" rather than independent per-tier heuristic rules — a candidate reframing for chartgen's own Easy→Expert derivation, especially since the same paper found adding an explicit fingering-motion term specifically improved reduction quality on harder pieces.

### Karaoke/vocal-alignment research (chartgen's lyrics feature)
- **Bimodal failure mode is the load-bearing finding**: forced-alignment models trained on solo vocals catastrophically fail on real mixed audio (mean absolute error 36.2s/39.7s, Gao et al. arXiv:1906.10369) but after domain adaptation, *median* error drops to 0.15–0.18s while *mean* stays much higher (0.93–1.93s) — most words align fine, a small subset go catastrophically and permanently wrong, almost always across long instrumental interludes where the aligner loses its anchor and never recovers. The fix documented across multiple sources is **periodic re-anchoring**, not a smarter single global pass.
- **Source separation before alignment is not automatically beneficial** — a mix-aware model beat a vocal-extracted one across all three tested datasets (arXiv:1909.10200: e.g. 3.62s→0.25s mean error on one set), while a different paper found separation "critical" for its own low-resource pipeline. The effect is pipeline-dependent; must be A/B tested, not assumed.
- **Vocal synthesis convention (Synthesizer V, VOCALOID, UTAU `oto.ini`)**: a note/timing marker should align to **vowel onset, not consonant onset** — the consonant is a documented "preutterance" that precedes the beat, with UTAU giving concrete numeric conventions (VCV preutterance ≈300ms/overlap ≈100ms; shorter VC ≈200ms/50ms). Directly useful if chartgen ever cross-checks LRCLIB timestamps against detected vocal onsets.
- **UltraStar format**: explicit `F`(reestyle)/`R`(ap) note categories, ungraded and unpitched, as a first-class distinct type rather than forcing talk-singing through a pitch-confidence continuum; phrase-break is an explicit authored token with hard structural rules (never inside a note span, never adjacent to another break).
- **Netflix Timed Text Style Guide** (official, published): 20 CPS (characters/sec) reading-speed cap for adult content, 0.83s minimum / 7s maximum event duration, asymmetric lead/lag sync tolerance, 2-frame minimum inter-event gap — a fully numeric, industry-published template for text-timing-to-audio decisions, orthogonal to RBN's breath/2-measure musical-phrase rule.
- MIREX's standard 0.3s alignment tolerance was **never perceptually validated** (Deezer/ISMIR 2021, arXiv via research.deezer.com) — tolerance is asymmetric and rhythm-context-dependent, not a flat constant; don't import 0.3s as a target without adapting it.

### MIR structure segmentation & solo/lead detection research
- **Solo detection from mixed audio remains a hard, small-data, non-generalizing problem** — the only dedicated academic solo detector (Pati & Lerch, AES 2017) reaches 67.3% F-measure on its own genre-matched dataset, but an independent 2025 re-test on a different dataset (arXiv:2503.03232) saw that collapse to **26% F1** — the technique does not generalize across recording conditions. A stronger model (82–87% F1) exists but *requires isolated multitrack stems*, architecturally inapplicable to chartgen's single mixed-audio input.
- Pati & Lerch's own numbers are a useful cross-check for chartgen's empirical solo prior: **26% of audio-time is solo, median solo length 35 seconds** (chartgen's own finding: ~30% of songs have a solo, ~16 bars, 45–80% position — different units but same order of magnitude, independently derived).
- Structure segmentation (MSAF systematic study, 7 algorithms × multiple datasets): algorithm rankings are dataset- and tolerance-window-dependent (statistically confirmed, all p<0.01) — there is no single best boundary detector; even human annotators only agree with each other 66–78% of the time on where a boundary is.
- Goto's RefraiD chorus-finder (100 RWC songs, 80/100 correct with avg F-measure 0.938 among successes) relies on ~15% of its real-world accuracy coming from three explicit **hand-tuned structural priors** (chorus length range 7.7–40s; end-of-repeated-block bias; nested-half-length-repeat bias) — ablating these dropped accuracy from 80 to 68/100, evidence that hand-tuned heuristic priors materially help even a signal-based method.
- Supervised chorus detection (ByteDance, arXiv:2103.14253) needed 900–2500+ labeled songs to beat unsupervised baselines by ≥0.14 F1 — an investment scale chartgen's 800-chart corpus (charts, not solo-boundary-labeled audio) doesn't currently support for this specific task.
- Correlation Block-Matching (CBM, TISMIR 2023) operates at **bar-scale** self-similarity and is unsupervised (no training data needed) — the most promising *primitive* if chartgen ever wants to detect riff/pattern repeats within a song for readability/muscle-memory-consistent charting, though this specific use (sub-phrase repeat detection rather than whole-section boundaries) is unvalidated repurposing, not a proven result.

## 4. Ideas explicitly rejected, with reasons

Organized by why they fail to transfer, not just restating "doesn't apply."

**Wrong physical geometry (biomechanics don't map to a 5-fret controller):**
- Beat Saber's swing-angle/curvature/tension-apex geometry (3D wrist rotation, bimanual independent paths) — no analog on a 5-lane fretboard; only the *methodology* (transition-based state tracking) transfers.
- ITG's technical-notation categories (crossovers, footswitches, brackets) are defined by dance-pad biomechanics (which foot, crossing the body's centerline) with no guitar-neck analog; only the *meta-pattern* (a formal notation for technique presence) transfers.
- CSP/biomechanical fingering models (Radicioni & Lombardo) assume real per-finger independence and hand-span-in-centimeters on a continuous 6-string×20-fret grid — GRYBO's single-row 5-button controller has no analogous 2D geometry or finger-crossing physics.
- osu!mania's literal numeric chord/jack caps are tuned to independent-finger keyboard biomechanics, not guitar-neck/strum-hand biomechanics — the *shape* (caps scale with tier) is already what chartgen's empirical tier-ratio work does; the specific numbers would be miscalibrated if imported.

**Requires infrastructure/data chartgen doesn't have:**
- BiRating's/BeatLeader's score-based difficulty inference — requires millions of aggregate player scores per map (a live ranked-leaderboard ecosystem); chartgen has no player-performance telemetry to bootstrap from. The *method*, not just the result, is inapplicable.
- Full audio-to-tab transcription models (TabCNN, FretNet, Fretting-Transformer) solve "which of 6 strings × ~20 frets" — a pitch-tied disambiguation problem that doesn't exist when lanes aren't tied to pitch classes at all; their 72–98% accuracy numbers are not informative benchmarks for CH lane assignment.
- Lead-instrument detection at usable accuracy (82–92% F1) requires isolated multitrack stems, not mixed audio — a real capability gap, not a technique chartgen can adopt today.
- Ubisoft's ARCHI and Rocksmith+'s "no simplification, maximal transcription accuracy" philosophy are product-positioning choices for a paid teaching product with human QA staff — inapplicable to a fully-automated tool whose entire purpose is a deliberately-simplified tiered ladder.

**Legally/structurally settled as "nothing to mine here":**
- Rocksmith's patented dynamic-difficulty mechanism was ruled by two courts (E.D.N.C., affirmed by the Federal Circuit, *Ubisoft v. Yousician*) to be nothing more than generic "assess performance, adjust a frequency/speed parameter" — no hidden sophisticated technique exists to reverse-engineer; the sophistication (if any) lives in unpublished tuning data.
- osu!'s and ScoreSaber/BeatLeader's star-rating formulas are either undocumented in prose (osu!, literal `<!-- TODO -->` on the official wiki) or fully private (ScoreSaber/BeatLeader) — there is no method here to borrow beyond the architectural pattern already extracted (§3, osu! section).
- Konami's IIDX/SDVX/DDR radar-axis formulas are never publicly disclosed by the developer — only the *concept* of multi-axis profiling is recoverable, not the math.
- SEGA's maimai/Chunithm internal difficulty constants are undisclosed; every public number is community reverse-engineering, not a spec.

**Sounds like it should transfer but the domain-defining assumption breaks:**
- GenerationMania's keysounded-charting techniques (Beatmania IIDX) — the chart *is* the sound design (player-triggered samples reconstruct the song); Clone Hero's backing track always plays regardless of player input, so nothing in this architecture transfers. TaikoNation's authors made the identical exclusion for the same reason.
- Fortnite Festival's non-Pro "Lift Note" model (press-or-release, no strum) and Encore's "lift" note type are real, documented third input primitives — but Clone Hero's `.chart`/MIDI format has no release-triggered note type at all, so nothing here is emittable into a CH-targeted chart without inventing new format syntax.
- FNF's "Erect/Nightmare" difficulty tiers work by *remixing the source audio itself* (retempo, new instrumentation) to manufacture a harder tier — chartgen's pipeline has a fixed audio input with no re-orchestration step; the mechanism requires a production capability that doesn't exist here.
- A Dance of Fire and Ice's angle-encodes-rhythm mechanic is the purest example of "encode subdivision without adding inputs," but it works only because the game's *entire notation* is the angle geometry itself; there's no way to graft this onto CH's existing 5-lane+sustain+HOPO notation without breaking format compatibility. Only the underlying *principle* (swing/subdivision as its own difficulty axis, independent of note count) survives.
- Rocksmith 1's "Combo" arrangement (continuously switch to whichever real part is musically prominent) vs. Rock Band's rigid single-path model is a genuine unresolved tradeoff even for the original team — worth knowing as a calibration of ambition (don't expect a clean universal answer to exist for "which part survives" reduction problems) rather than as a technique to adopt.

**Right instinct, wrong evidence/generalization:**
- Pitch/timbre-driven lane assignment as a *general* rule — osu!mania's own community folklore explicitly recommends against mapping "the whole song" to pitch (ergonomic imbalance), reserving it for sparse/calm sections only; osu!taiko's version (pitch informs which of 2 colors, with anti-repetition) is the more disciplined model if chartgen ever explores this, not a blanket pitch→lane mapping.
- Using generic MIR structure segmentation as a *feature* for solo detection — already tried (Pati & Lerch) and found to *underperform* plain audio features alone (F 40.3% vs 59.8%) in isolation; segmentation is built to find repeats, but solos are defined by non-repetition and instrumental foregrounding, arguably the wrong tool family.
- Treating any single MSAF segmentation algorithm as "the" baseline — the systematic study's central finding is there is no universally-best algorithm; picking one off a leaderboard without matching genre (chartgen's corpus skews rock/metal, underrepresented in the standard SALAMI/Isophonics/Beatles benchmarks) risks importing a tool tuned for a different musical population.
- Generic ASR-grade forced aligners (stock Gentle, stock Montreal Forced Aligner) as a drop-in lyric-timing refinement over full mixes — multiple independent sources document these fail badly on singing without domain-specific retraining (MFA singing study; Gao et al.'s 36–40s catastrophic-error numbers); a real technical trap, not folklore to dismiss.
- DAMP as a training/validation proxy for chartgen's actual (full-mix) input — it's 100% solo a cappella; models fine-tuned only on it are exactly the ones that fail catastrophically (36–40s mean error) on real mixed tracks.

**Cosmetic/governance, no algorithmic content:**
- osu!'s Beatmap Nominator human-review process (hype votes, veto politics, 7-day cooldowns) — a multi-stakeholder taste-arbitration system chartgen (single automated tool, no community taste disputes) has no structural need for; only the narrow "late finding invalidates prior approval, scoped locally" idea survives.
- Difficulty-naming scheme flexibility (Easy/Normal/Hard vs. Kantan/Futsuu vs. Cup/Salad/Platter) — purely cosmetic, zero algorithmic content.
- BeatSaver's 90-day AI-content retention policy — a platform/social/authorship-attribution decision, not a technical finding about generation quality.
- Beat Saber's lighting-as-expression material — even BSMG's own wiki calls it "much more subjective than mapping" with no rigorous theory to extract, and there's no lighting channel in a guitar chart to design for anyway.
- RBN's Big Rock Ending mechanic — fully specified but tied to Rock Band's scripted freeform-jam end-of-song business logic; Clone Hero has no BRE concept and no generalizable "ending" heuristic beyond what chartgen presumably already does.
- The Rocksmith patent litigation itself, and Rock Band DLC project-management/codename logistics — real history, zero charting-content implication beyond what's already extracted elsewhere.
